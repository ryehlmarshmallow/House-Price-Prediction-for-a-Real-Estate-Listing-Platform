2. Input and prediction drift

The credit-market variables moved outside the reference support during the monitoring period: mortgage rates fell from a reference median of roughly 6.5 percent to roughly 5.0 percent, and the local price index declined from about 104 to 96. PSI values far above the 0.20 threshold therefore reflect non-overlapping distributions rather than a modelling error. 3. Accuracy and calibration

Accuracy is stable in the first two windows and deteriorates modestly in the last two, where the model systematically under-predicts realised prices by up to five percent. The consequence is visible in the calibration panel: the nominal 90 percent interval covers only 80 percent of sales in the final window. Interval recalibration is therefore the first corrective action, before a full retraining cycle.

To verify that the alerting machinery reacts to a sharper regime change, the same model was scored on a counterfactual stream in which the mortgage rate rises by 1.5 percentage points, inventory rises by one month of supply, and neighbourhood amenity distributions improve by 60 percent. Recorded prices are held at their observed values; the scenario therefore measures sensitivity to context shift, not a forecast of future accuracy.

6. Methodology and limitations

  • Reference model: the selected estimator refitted on 945 sales completed before 2009; out-of-fold RMSE 20,888 USD.
  • Windows: four equal-volume chronological blocks, so that every RMSE and PSI estimate rests on roughly the same number of sales.
  • Drift: PSI on reference quantile bins (numeric) or level proportions (categorical), plus two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov for numeric features.
  • Uncertainty: split-conformal intervals calibrated on the reference vintage; coverage is measured against realised sales, which is the only honest test of calibration.
  • The simulation uses recorded Ames sales as delayed labels. It cannot reproduce seasonality beyond the sampled period, and the stress scenario holds prices fixed.
